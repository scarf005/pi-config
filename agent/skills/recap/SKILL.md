# Recap

Restate in your own words what you think the user's goals are and what problem they are trying to solve.

- Ground the recap in the conversation, including corrections and explicit constraints. Distinguish the desired outcome from the requested implementation and the underlying problem from its symptoms.
- Briefly explain what success would look like and any constraints that materially affect it. Do not invent motives, requirements, technical root causes, or agreement; label uncertain interpretations as such.
- Do not substitute a task log, change list, or generic conversation summary for the user's goals and problem.
- Use concise Korean honorifics for this user, normally 2–4 sentences. Use a different format or level of detail when explicitly requested.
- If work has finished, separately connect the observed result to that goal and state any unmet criterion or unverified behavior without claiming success beyond the evidence.
- Recapping alone does not authorize edits, commands, new tasks, or continuation. Ask a focused question only if a material ambiguity blocks alignment; do not routinely ask for confirmation.

When recapping at task completion without an explicit invocation, compress the goals and problem into 1–2 sentences alongside the ordinary result and validation report. Respect exact-output constraints; omit the recap when it would violate a requested schema, artifact-only response, or brevity requirement.
